## Reading the results

**Values describe a typical plot, not the whole cell.** An FIA plot covers about 0.07 hectares (about 1/6 acre), while grid cells are 1 to 100 km² depending on the selected resolution. Map values are what you would expect on a plot-sized patch of forest in that cell: for example, the chance that a species is present on such a patch, or its basal area per acre there. They are not totals for the cell, and they should not be added up across cells to estimate totals for an area.

**Some values rest on more evidence than others.** The `Effective sample size` map layer shows roughly how many plots each cell's value effectively rests on. Low values mean thin evidence. Projections into "non-analog" climates with few close matches on today's landscape are especially uncertain.

**Projections are not predictions.** Future maps show what today's forests suggest about a projected climate, for one emissions pathway, time period, and set of climate models. They leave out many things that will shape real outcomes, including disturbance, management, dispersal, competition, pests, and the direct effects of carbon dioxide. Different climate models can disagree; the `Climate models` panel shows how much.

**Ratios near zero.** Measures that are ratios, such as evenness or regeneration fractions, take extreme values where the species or community is barely present. The support floor setting hides cells where there is too little to measure.

**Use it alongside other knowledge.** TreeShift is most useful for comparing places and scenarios and for prompting questions, read together with local expertise, site data, and other models.
